# Water Softener Guide

## How it works (plain language)

Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. A softener sends water through a tank of resin beads that grab those minerals and swap in sodium (or potassium). When the beads are full, the softener **regenerates**: it flushes salty brine from the salt tank through the beads to recharge them and sends the rinse water down a drain line. Regeneration usually happens at night (often 2 a.m.) and takes 1–2 hours.

Parts:
- **Resin tank**: the tall, narrow tank.
- **Control head (valve)**: the top of the resin tank; has the display, clock, and settings.
- **Brine tank**: the wider bin you pour salt into.
- **Bypass valve**: on the pipes behind the control head; sends water around the softener when set to bypass.
- **Drain line**: small hose from the control head to a floor drain or standpipe.

## Is it working? Checks in order

1. **Power and clock.** Is the display on? Is the time correct? A power outage often resets the clock (or flashes the time), which makes it regenerate at the wrong time. Error codes usually appear here; look up the code in the model's manual.
2. **Bypass valve.** It should be set to *service* (normal), not *bypass*. On push-button or knob valves, check the arrows or label. Kids, movers, or plumbers sometimes leave it on bypass.
3. **Salt level.** The brine tank should be at least ¼ full, with salt above the water line. Note the level now and check again in 2–4 weeks; it should go down. If the salt never drops, the softener isn't using it.
4. **Salt bridge.** A hard crust can form with empty space underneath, so it looks full but isn't making brine. Push a broom handle down through the salt gently. If it hits a hard layer that breaks, it was bridged; break it up and the system should recover after the next regeneration.
5. **Hardness test.** Buy hardness test strips (cheap at hardware stores). Test water from a softened tap (kitchen or bathroom) and from an unsoftened source (usually the outdoor spigot, sometimes a separate kitchen cold tap). Softened water should read near zero, within 1–3 grains per gallon; unsoftened will be much higher. If both read the same, it's not softening.
6. **Manual regeneration.** Most units have a "regen" or "recharge" button (often held for a few seconds). Start one and listen: you should hear water running and the drain line should flow within a few minutes. No sound and no drain flow means a stuck valve, clogged injector, or kinked drain line.
7. **Settings.** Check the hardness setting on the control head against the actual incoming hardness (from the outdoor-spigot test strip, or the local water utility's published hardness). If it's set too low, the softener regenerates too rarely and hard water slips through.

## Sensory clues

- **Soft:** soap lathers easily, skin feels slippery after a shower, fewer spots on dishes.
- **Hard:** white crust on faucets and showerheads, spotty glasses, soap scum, stiff laundry.

## Common problems

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Salt never goes down | Salt bridge, not regenerating, clock reset, bypass on | Break bridge; check clock and bypass; run a manual regen |
| Water in brine tank is very high | Clogged injector/screen, kinked drain line, float issue | Check drain line for kinks; clean injector per manual; call a pro if it persists |
| Hard water but salt is fine | Hardness setting too low, resin worn out (10–15+ years), bypass partly open | Raise hardness setting; test; consider resin replacement |
| Salty-tasting water | Regeneration not rinsing fully, too much brine, drain restriction | Check drain line; check settings; call a pro if it persists |
| Low water pressure | Resin fouled or crushed, clogged pre-filter | Check any sediment filter; pro service |
| Runs all the time / drain never stops | Stuck valve or motor | Unplug, put in bypass, call a pro |

## Maintenance

- **Check salt:** monthly; top up to about half full. Don't fill to the brim.
- **Salt type:** use what the manual says (usually pellets, or solar salt). Avoid rock salt, which leaves sludge.
- **Check for a salt bridge:** every 2–3 months, especially in humid summers.
- **Clean the brine tank:** about once a year if there's sludge at the bottom, or every few years otherwise.
- **Resin cleaner:** every few months on well water or if there's iron; follow the product directions.
- **Re-check hardness:** a few times a year with test strips.
- **Lifespan:** control heads often last 10–15 years; resin 10–20 years depending on water and chlorine.

## When to call a pro

- Brine tank keeps overflowing or flooding.
- Valve or motor won't cycle, or it runs constantly.
- Still hard after a bridge fix, correct settings, and a manual regen.
- It's 15+ years old and needs a major part: get a repair vs. replace quote.

Look for a water treatment dealer or plumber who services the specific brand. Many softeners in Canada are rentals; if there's a sticker from a rental company, the user may be able to call them for free service.
